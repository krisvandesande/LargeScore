# Hand check on a real iPad

What simulators can't tell us: an evening with the real player, at their size, in their light, with their pedal. Note the date, the iPad, the size step and what went wrong.

## Before

- The iPad on the stand at playing distance, in the room's usual light.
- The pedal paired (Settings › Bluetooth) and Large Score open on Today's programme (in Programmes).
- A programme of three pieces, one of them twice (an encore), and one piece with repeats.

## Reading

1. Read the first page at the chosen size. Can every note, accidental and dynamic be read without leaning in? If not, one size up, and note it.
2. Turn with the pedal, a tap and a swipe. Is the first bar's tint seen, and does it help or distract?
3. Turn quickly twice with the pedal at the end of a piece: the next piece must show, not the one after.
4. Play through the encore: the same piece opens again at its first page.
5. With Repeats in Playing Order on, play the piece with repeats: the pages only go forward, and the music matches what is played.
6. With Key Reminders on: do the extra sharps and flats help, or clutter?
7. Try Yellow on Black and White on Black in the evening light.
8. Give one busy piece its own size from the library, and check the others keep theirs.

## During a rehearsal

9. Add a piece to Today's programme while the reader is open on another iPad or the Mac: the open reader must not jump.
10. Write a note with Apple Pencil on a bar, then change the size: the note stays on its bar.
11. Lock the iPad for ten minutes, unlock: the pedal still turns pages.

## Voice Control and VoiceOver (briefly)

12. Say "Tap Library", "Tap Notes", "Next Page" (Voice Control).
13. With VoiceOver, swipe through the reader: each page is announced with its bars.

## Afterwards

Write what was found into `docs/accessibility-findings.md`, with the date and the iPad.
